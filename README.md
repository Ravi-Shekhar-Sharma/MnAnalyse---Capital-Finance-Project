MnAnalyse: Air India–Vistara Merger and SIA Strategic Assessment

Overview

This repository contains a strategic and financial assessment of the Air India–Vistara merger, with a focus on Singapore Airlines' position in the merged Air India entity.

The presentation studies the transaction from several angles, including strategic fit, operating synergies, valuation, regulatory approvals, integration risks, stakeholder impact, ESG priorities, customer experience, and post-merger execution.

The analysis was prepared in August 2025 as part of the MnAnalyse project.

What the project examines

The presentation addresses the following questions:

Why does the merger make strategic sense?

Where can cost and revenue synergies come from?

How long could it take for the transaction to break even?

What could the merged airline be worth under the stated assumptions?

What is the potential value of Singapore Airlines' 25.1% stake?

Which regulatory, operational, cultural, and technology risks may affect integration?

What should management prioritise during the first five years after the merger?

Scope of analysis

Strategic assessment

The project reviews the combined airline's potential advantages in:

domestic and international network coverage

premium and full-service market positioning

fleet and procurement efficiencies

loyalty programme integration

brand strength and customer retention

global alliances and code-share opportunities

Financial analysis

The presentation includes:

a five-year operating forecast

a discounted cash flow model

a break-even assessment

cost and revenue synergy estimates

an NPV calculation for expected synergies

a valuation bridge from pre-merger to post-merger enterprise value

an illustrative valuation of Singapore Airlines' stake

a comparable-company review of global airline peers

Under the assumptions used in the presentation, the model indicates break-even around the third year and an illustrative post-merger enterprise value of approximately ₹71,147 crore.

Risk and regulatory review

The project maps the main approval and compliance areas involving bodies such as:

Competition Commission of India

Directorate General of Civil Aviation

Ministry of Civil Aviation

Securities and Exchange Board of India

relevant international regulators

It also includes an integration risk heatmap covering fleet harmonisation, cultural alignment, IT systems, loyalty programme migration, regulatory delays, and synergy cost overruns.

Stakeholders and ESG

The analysis considers the expected impact on:

shareholders

employees

customers

regulators

local communities

It also outlines a five-year ESG roadmap covering fleet age, sustainable aviation fuel, operational efficiency, governance, emissions disclosure, and workforce retention.

Customer experience roadmap

The proposed roadmap includes:

alignment of service standards

integration of loyalty programmes

fleet-wide Wi-Fi and entertainment upgrades

expansion of premium economy

better lounge access

personalised customer support

biometric boarding and baggage tracking

Post-merger execution plan

The recommended implementation plan is divided into three phases:

Stabilise, 0 to 12 monthsComplete operational integration, align service standards, migrate loyalty systems, and protect reliability.

Optimise, 1 to 3 yearsCapture synergies, improve network planning, strengthen revenue management, and establish a unified brand.

Scale, beyond 3 yearsExpand international capacity, deepen alliance relationships, and improve the airline's global position.

Key takeaway

The presentation argues that the merger can create value through scale, network depth, premium positioning, procurement savings, and stronger customer retention. At the same time, the expected value depends on disciplined integration, timely regulatory approvals, realistic synergy capture, and consistent service delivery.
